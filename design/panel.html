A compressor that looks like it has a power switch on the back

Walnut cheeks, anodised aluminium, engraved bays and a backlit window. Every control below maps to a parameter that already works in the plugin — this is a skin over Phase 4, not a wishlist. Graphite is the chosen finish; the other two are kept here as the alternatives that were considered. The geometry is identical across all three.

1180 × 460 pt. Meters animate to show how gain reduction reads in motion.

Decisions worth your veto

  • Bay widths are computed, not eyeballed. A knob's real footprint is 2 × (r + 11), because its tick arc reaches widest at 3 and 9 o'clock — not at the pointer. Every bay is sized from the footprints it contains plus a 12 pt gap between neighbours and 14 pt to each divider. Sizing by eye is what put tick arcs through the dividers in the first pass, and it is why the panel had to grow to 1180 pt: twenty controls genuinely do not fit 1000 with honest clearance.
  • Ratio is the hero, not threshold. It is the control that changes what the unit is — and at 1:1 it is also the transparency switch, so it earns the biggest knob.
  • Two tiers, not one. Twenty controls in a single row would mean twenty tiny knobs. The upper tier is what you touch while mixing; the lower strip is setup you configure once.
  • The window is amber, not a screen. It reads as backlit glass rather than an LCD, which keeps it in the same material world as the rest of the box.
  • The panel ignores your OS theme. A piece of hardware does not change colour when the sun goes down. The page around it responds; the unit does not.
